# Noise checklist — what the second hand cuts

From the copyedit of the Ring book and the readers' tics lists. `tools/style_lint.py` finds most of these; the rest are read.

**Found by the lint**
- Calques and homophones: hole/whole, staff/stuff, witch/which, fellow/follow, then/than, quiet/quite, "in the same time", "from one hand", "obeys to", "a useful information", "in plus of".
- Intensifiers: very, really, simply, obviously, obvious, dead simple, "as simple as that", "very simple", "so easy".
- Hedge stacks: "maybe, and probably", "may be" (for maybe), "somehow", "kind of", "a little bit".
- Trailing ellipses at the end of a paragraph; colon lead-ins ("like this:", "Result:") that end a paragraph before a non-code paragraph.
- Superlatives and self-advertising: "one of the most", "for the first time", "first ever", "unprecedented", "revolutionary", "game-changing", "paradigm shift", "world-class", "state of the art".
- Unbacked absolutes: "the root cause of all", "all problems", "every programmer", "no other language", "never" / "always" on a claim (counted, for a reader to judge).
- Forbidden words: substrate; maker (outside decision-maker); leverage (verb), utilize, best practice, stakeholders; developer (for programmer); emojis.
- Long sentences over 40 words (one in thirty is the profile); exclamations over the profile or on an abstract sentence; em dashes over the profile.

**Read, not linted**
- Scope inflation in a promise ("how to contribute the official codebase" learned in chapter 1).
- A run-on roadmap (more than five sentences of plan).
- A listing repeated in full after an incremental change.
- "The next table presents" and other lead-ins repeated more than three times in a chapter.
- A fact slip (a wrong term for an error, a misspelt name, a miscomputed number): the verifier's job.
- Drift in an invented name (Ringopus / Ringapus): the audit's job.
- Mixed grammar inside one list.

**Not noise (the author's calls, see FRAMEWORK §4):** the tool as "she", the flourish, the trailing ellipsis as a hand-over to code (one per section), the question "Agreed?", the exclamation after the run, the foreign word.
