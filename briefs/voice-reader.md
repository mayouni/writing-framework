# Brief: read a chapter for its voice

You are a fresh reader. You did not write this chapter. You are given the chapter (and, if the book has one, its `CLAUDE.md` voice section and the framework's `FRAMEWORK.md` §2). You change nothing; you report.

The question is not "is it well written" but **"is it his"**: does the page keep the twelve commitments of the AYOUNI Writing Framework, and where does it sound like an agent instead of the author? Evidence for every point: a verbatim quote under 20 words with the heading it sits under. A pattern is something seen three times or once in a place that matters (an opening, a close). No praise, no generalities.

Report (your final message, under 1,000 words):

1. **Shape (C9).** The opening: does it say what the chapter will do, in the first person, in a few sentences? Each section's close: a hand-over or a dead end? The chapter's close: bridge, release, homework, coda, or a summary of what was just said?
2. **Run, then read (C1).** Where does an explanation come before the thing it explains? Where is a paragraph of abstract nouns with no run, figure or example within reach? Quote the worst one.
3. **The defect (C2).** What does the chapter leave in the reader's hands, named as unfinished? Is the exercise a real defect or a demonstration? Is a wrong version ever shown before the right one? Is correct code ever rejected for a reason of taste, with the reason?
4. **Where it stops (C3).** Is the limit stated, with stage words? Is a loss printed beside each win? List every claim that sounds larger than what the page shows (you are not verifying facts; you are hearing reach).
5. **The particulars (C4).** List every proper name, date, place, person and memory. For each: is it sourced on the page or in the record, or does it read as composed? Any `[AUTHOR` markers left? Any sentence that puts a memory, a feeling or a motive into the author's mouth?
6. **Metaphors (C5).** List them. Which carry weight (explain what no code there explains)? Which are decorative (deletable without loss)? Is a sustained metaphor mapped term by term where it starts? Do its names stay stable?
7. **Address (C6).** The jobs of I / we / you on this page, with counts if you can; questions: are they beats answered within the page, or rhetoric? Exclamations: after a run or a reveal, or on an abstract sentence? Is the tool ever quoted as speaking?
8. **Rhythm and noise (C7, §4).** Long-then-short present? Colon or dash? Intensifiers (very, really, simply, just, obvious), hedges, trailing ellipses, superlatives ("one of the most", "for the first time"), unbacked absolutes ("all", "never", "the root cause"), calques, forbidden words (substrate; maker; the field's hype words). Give counts and three examples.
9. **Counted sets (C8).** The named sets the chapter uses; does each stated count match its list; are the names exact?
10. **The stake (C10).** Where does the chapter rise above the technical point? Is that claim backed on the page, marked as belief, or neither?
11. **Three passages that are most him**, quoted (under 60 words each), and **one that is least him**, quoted, with the reason.
12. **The one change** that would do the most for the voice of this chapter, in one sentence.
